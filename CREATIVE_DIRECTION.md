# Senseiuc Creative Direction

## Core Idea

Senseiuc is an engineering field notebook shaped by strategic thinking. It should feel like Gideon annotated real systems on a dark drafting table, with a recurring mentor character guiding the reader through decisions and evidence.

The site is not a cyberpunk dashboard, a terminal theme, or a generic dark developer portfolio.

## Visual Grammar

- **Ink:** near-black surfaces with visible structure, not soft floating glass.
- **Signal:** orange marks decisions, active paths, and measured outcomes.
- **Evidence:** green is reserved for verified success states; cool gray supports infrastructure diagrams.
- **Grid:** use the 64px systems grid, rules, coordinates, and numbered moves to organize content.
- **Character:** mascots explain a role or state. They do not appear as arbitrary stickers.
- **Type:** Outfit carries the human narrative. JetBrains Mono labels evidence, state, and system metadata.
- **Shape:** square corners and thin rules dominate. Rounded containers are reserved for controls, dialogs, and compact tools.
- **Motion:** slow idle character movement and directional system flow. No ambient bouncing, spinning icons, or decorative motion without meaning.

## Page Rhythm

Alternate between four distinct compositions:

1. Editorial statement with a strong claim.
2. Evidence row with measured outcomes.
3. Annotated system diagram or interactive model.
4. Human context using the mascot, portrait, or project artifact.

Avoid stacking multiple grids of identical cards.

## Mascot Rules

- Ledger: financial integrity, reconciliation, and payouts.
- Integration: APIs, webhooks, migrations, and connected platforms.
- Architect: system boundaries, concurrency, and failure planning.
- Code, cloud, secure, deploy, and debug: only beside their matching capability or state.
- Teach, math, think, and Nigeria: personal context, education, and location.
- Keep no more than one animated mascot group in a viewport where practical.

## Copy Rules

- Lead with the operational problem, not the technology list.
- Prefer concrete verbs: built, reduced, migrated, protected, recovered, reconciled.
- Pair every metric with what changed and the conditions around it.
- Avoid invented system language such as “neural core,” “compute pod daemon,” or “transmission node” when ordinary language is clearer.
- Preserve confidentiality and distinguish production evidence from demonstrations.

## Avoid

- Gradient text and gradient buttons.
- Glowing or blurred decorative blobs.
- Rainbow accents assigned arbitrarily per card or project.
- Tiny body copy used to simulate technical density.
- Repeated glass cards with the same radius and shadow.
- Fake terminal language outside the actual terminal feature.
- Mascots used only to fill empty space.
- Generic claims such as “innovative solutions” or “cutting-edge technology.”

## Review Checklist

- Could this section belong to another developer by changing the name?
- Does every visual element communicate identity, evidence, hierarchy, or state?
- Is the mascot performing a role relevant to the adjacent content?
- Is body copy comfortably readable on a phone?
- Does the section use a different rhythm from the one immediately before it?
- Are technical claims supported by context or explicitly qualified?
